Rule six is about something unusual: in our kitchen the chef is often asleep, literally, because the cooks work at night. A normal assistant would stop and wait for an answer: “Spicy or mild?” By morning nothing would be done. Our cooks are told: don’t wait. Make the most sensible choice and leave a note explaining it. The first two notes are exactly that. In the morning I read the notes and either say “fine” or “change that”, and a cook redoes it. Only when a decision really can’t be guessed, like the allergy on the third note, does a cook park the ticket and ask for a human. Nobody else will touch that ticket until a person answers.
